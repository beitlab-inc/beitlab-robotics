Balance Robot v1

Prototype build

A wheeled biped with four-bar legs. One hip servo per leg sets the height and the wheel stays under the hip; two direct-drive brushless motors keep it upright.

  • Four-bar leg: wheel within 0.8 mm of under the hip, 15–55° hip range
  • GM4108H gimbal motors with field-oriented control, no gearbox backlash
  • 3D-printed body (PLA/PETG), 4S LiPo, top-mounted E-stop
  • URDF, ROS 2 description and Isaac Lab tasks generated from the CAD

Robotics Board v0.8

Design frozen

A carrier board for the Milk-V Duo S that puts motor drivers, power conversion, IMU and the safety chain on one 4-layer PCB. No external microcontroller is needed.

  • 2 × DRV8313 three-phase drivers, 5.3 V / 5 A and 6 V / 8 A bucks
  • BMI323 IMU, ADS1115 rail monitor, four SPI angle-sensor ports
  • Latched hardware E-stop and watchdog: motors stay off until armed
  • Status LEDs and test points on every safety signal

What we focus on

Four engineering principles.

Agile robots fail in the gaps between disciplines. We close those gaps by owning every layer and checking each one automatically.

Hard real-time on open silicon

The SG2000's C906 RISC-V core runs FreeRTOS with FOC at 10 kHz and the balance loop at 400 Hz. Linux and ROS 2 run beside it on the Cortex-A53.

Safety in hardware

Motor enable is MOT_EN_REQ AND a watchdog AND a latched E-stop. The E-stop also switches off the motor supply, and the board powers up safe.

Everything generated, everything checked

The schematic and PCB come from Python, the body from CadQuery, and the URDF and USD from the CAD. ERC, DRC, interference sweeps and closed-loop tests run on every change.

Simulation first

The firmware controller runs in the loop against physics, and Isaac Lab trains policies across thousands of parallel robots, which are compared with the LQR baseline before hardware.

System architecture

Two cores, one board, no external MCU.

Balance never waits on Linux. Linux sends commands and reads state through fixed-size rings in shared memory; the real-time core owns every sensor and actuator.
