Robotics Board

Robotics Board v0.11

A carrier board for the Milk-V Duo S that connects every robot device to the SG2000's C906 RISC-V core. The motor drivers, power conversion, IMU and safety chain are all on board; motors, sensors and servos plug in by cable.

Architecture

There is no external microcontroller. The SG2000's C906 little core runs FreeRTOS: FOC for both wheels in a 10 kHz fast interrupt, the 400 Hz balance loop and the hip servos. Linux on the Cortex-A53 runs ROS 2 and talks to it through shared memory. The board routes every real-time signal to pins the C906 can own.

The right wheel runs on PWM controller 0 (3.3 V header J3) and the left wheel on controller 2 (1.8 V header J4, through a level translator), so each wheel's three phases update together. The hip servos use the remaining hardware PWM. No pad is left for the head, so a PCA9685 on I²C4 makes the J27 and J28 pulses. All four angle sensors share SPI3.

Plugging in the battery

From v0.10, connect the pack with the ON/OFF switch at OFF (from v0.11, the rocker in the cover): the master MOSFET then keeps the board's input capacitance from charging at the XT30. The capacitance charges when you switch ON instead, and that turn-on current is not limited; measure it on the bench before relying on it. On v0.5–v0.9 boards about 260 µF charges through Q1's body diode when the pack is connected, so expect a spark at the XT30: use an anti-spark lead, a 10 Ω pre-charge resistor bridged out after a second, or a battery switch that is closed last.

Switching OFF cuts power to the Duo S at once. Shut Ubuntu down and wait for it to halt first. The E-stop cuts motor power, not the battery, and the ON/OFF switch is not an emergency stop. J26 is the same connector as J25: if the two leads are swapped, the rocker shorts J25's current-limited output when ON and the robot does not switch on, so label them.

Torque budget. The GM4108H is a direct-drive gimbal motor. With the firmware's 1.5 A current clamp, expect about 0.15 N·m per wheel. With the robot's 84.8 mm wheels that is enough to recover from a lean of roughly 20°, not for fast get-ups: keep the robot light. There is no current sensing; torque is set through a motor model, so measure R, Ke and the zero electrical angle on every motor.

Safety chain

Motor enable is decided in hardware, independently of software:

MOT_EN = MOT_EN_REQ  AND  WDT_OK  AND  ESTOP_RUN     # 74LVC1G11 → both DRV8313 EN pins
  • Watchdog (WDT_OK). A 74LVC1G123 retriggerable one-shot of about 10 ms. The C906 toggles WDT_KICK on every 400 Hz balance tick. It triggers on edges, so a hung core holding the pin high or low drops EN within about 10 ms.
  • E-stop latch (ESTOP_RUN). A 74AUP1G74 flip-flop. Opening the normally-closed loop on J10, or unplugging it, clears the latch at once: EN drops and Q2 cuts VMOT within about 2 ms. A bouncing switch cannot set it again. Only a rising ESTOP_ARM edge from firmware, with MOT_EN_REQ high and the loop closed, re-arms it.
  • Safe at power-up. A supervisor (U15) holds the latch clear through startup and brownouts, and every control line has a pull-down. The drivers stay asleep and disabled while Linux boots.
  • Accessory output. J24 follows the E-stop latch by default (jumper JP1, pads 1–2). Moving it to 2–3 keeps J24 on whenever the board is powered: do that only deliberately.
  • The servo rail is not cut by the E-stop, because cutting knee power would drop the robot. The servo-hold policy on faults is a firmware decision.
  • Aux servo pulses. The PCA9685 output enable follows the E-stop latch, inverted. While the latch is clear, including at power-up, J27 and J28 stay low and those servos go limp. Their supply stays on, because it comes from the knee rail through D18 and D19.
