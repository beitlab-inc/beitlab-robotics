Before you print

First build: measure these first

A few dimensions in the CAD are taken from drawings or public models, not from the parts in your hands. Check them, update the values in the generator and rebuild the STLs (make body) if they differ.

  • Servo clamp arm: the arm plate's thickness (3 mm assumed), its seat height on the spline (0.5 mm assumed), the hole positions, and whether the holes are M3-threaded. Print one thigh and try it on the arm first.
  • DS3240 case: the ears (3.2 mm thick, 18.7 mm across, Ø 4.3 holes), the 40.2 × 20.2 mm case and the lead's grommet.
  • GM4108H: the base pattern (4 × M3 on Ø 26), the 24 mm base-to-rotor length, and room in the centre for the wheel cover's pin.

Print

PETG for the leg parts and the spacer; PLA is fine for the body. Use 0.2 mm layers, 4 walls and 40 % infill (100 % for the knee spacer). The STLs are already oriented for printing. _left files are the mirrored left-leg parts.

Assemble

  1. Inserts Press every heat-set insert first: an M5 into each base pivot boss, M3s into the base's rear wall, and the inserts in the frame, ear plates, deck, thighs, shins and wheel rims. The shin's sensor holes are only 5 mm deep: use the 4 mm short M3 inserts there.
  2. Body: frame on base The frame's four plate inserts go in from below, flush with the plate. Set the frame on the base and fix it with 4 × M3 × 45 from underneath, up through the base posts (2.5 mm hex). You can do this at any time, even with the servos in.
  3. Hip servos
    • Lay each servo on its side and push an ear plate onto the case from above, open side first, right under the ears. Don't slide it over the servo's lower end: the lead's grommet is in the way.
    • Drop the servo in from above, spline outward, about 3 mm inboard of its place, then push it outward until the ears sit flat on the pads. The rib on the front ear goes into its pocket.
    • From outside, drive 4 × M3 × 18 through the wall counterbores into the ear plate.
    • Power the servo to its centre pulse. Push the clamp arm onto the spline through the wall hole, pointing down and about 35° forward, and tighten its centre screw from outside and its clamp screws through the slot from above.
    Do the servos before you feed the battery lead.
  4. Hip: thigh on the clamp arm Press the thigh onto the arm so the arm sits in the pocket on its inner face. Fix it with 2 × M3 × 6 from the thigh's outer face. Not longer: the tips must stop short of the arm's inner face, because the wall is only 1 mm away.
  5. Knee Push the 29.5 mm knee shaft into the thigh, flush with its inner face, and tighten the M3 × 8 grub screw on its flat. Add the knee spacer. Press two 688ZZ into the shin from its outer face and slide the shin on. Put the Ø 6 × 2.5 magnet in its holder and glue the holder on the shaft end. The shin must turn freely with no end play.
  6. Bone Press a 625ZZ into each end from the side without the lip.
    • Pivot end: a washer on the boss, the bone, a washer in the lip hole, then an M5 × 12 button head from outside.
    • Shin end, from the thigh side: M5 × 14 with a washer, the bone's bearing, a washer in the lip hole, one more washer, then the shin's insert.
    Use medium threadlocker on both screws.
  7. Check by hand Turn the leg through its range (hip 15–55°). It should move as one piece, the wheel should stay under the hip, and nothing should rub. Look first at the bone-to-shin screw head, which passes 1.5 mm from the thigh's edge.
  8. Knee sensor Solder the SPI wires to the AS5048A board's top fingers. Fit the mount on the shin, drop the board in chip down, facing the magnet 1 mm away, put the cap on with its pins in the board's holes, and fix the stack with 2 × M3 × 18 into the short inserts.
  9. Wheel motors Fix the GM4108H to the shin with 4 × M3 × 8 from the inner-face counterbores (check the motor's thread depth first). Slide the wheel and TPU tread over the motor's rotating case. Fit the motor cover: 4 × M3 × 5 to the rotor face and 4 × M3 × 8 to the rim.
  10. Battery and electronics
    • Feed the battery lead up through the slot at the rear centre of the frame plate and the deck before the board goes on. Load the 4S pack from the rear on its velcro straps and fit the battery door (2 × M3 × 8).
    • Fix the deck with 4 × M3 × 8 into the frame's deck posts.
    • Mount the Duo S on its four M2.5 hex standoffs, then the Robotics Board on top, with 2 × M3 × 6 at H1/H2 into the deck bosses.

Before the first balance
  • The robot only goes on the floor after the board has passed every bring-up stage, including the battery soak.
  • Gains must be designed from measured motor constants and mass properties. There is intentionally no default gain table.
  • Keep the E-stop within reach and a physical battery disconnect in the lead. The E-stop cuts motor power, not the servos or the battery.
  • A hard fall loads the servo's own bearings directly. Start with a tether or a hand ready.
